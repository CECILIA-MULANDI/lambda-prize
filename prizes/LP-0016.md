# LP-0016: Private Voting App with Passport-Based Sybil Resistance [DRAFT]

**`Status: Draft`**
**`Logos Circle: N/A`**

## Overview

On-chain voting is either transparent (every vote is publicly attributable) or anonymous but trivially Sybil-attacked (one person, many votes). Neither is acceptable for high-stakes decisions — governance referenda, community resolutions, or civic polls — where both ballot secrecy and one-person-one-vote are hard requirements.

This prize funds a private voting application on LEZ that combines shielded ballot submission with passport-based Sybil resistance: voters prove they hold a valid, unique government-issued passport without revealing their identity, and cast a ballot that no observer — including the vote coordinator — can link back to them. The result is a voting system where participation is provably unique and ballots are provably secret, with no trusted intermediary.

## Motivation

Real-world deployments of passport-based private voting (notably Rarimo's FreedomTool, used by 40,000 participants in Russian opposition voting — the largest protest gathering in Russia since 2021) have surfaced hard lessons that whiteboard designs miss:

**Sybil resistance is a spectrum, not a checkbox.** Passport-based uniqueness has three known architectures, each with different trust and deployment trade-offs:

1. **Active Authentication** — the passport chip signs a challenge, yielding a deterministic nullifier. Clean, but major countries have dropped support for the standard since the 2010s. It does not scale globally.
2. **External signer** (TEE, OPRF, or trusted server) — an additional secret is introduced to derive the nullifier. This is the architecture that shipped in production for the Russian use case. It works, but introduces a vendor dependency that a hostile state can pressure or shut down.
3. **Shielded passport-hash registry** — passport hashes are committed to an on-chain registry and reused across use cases, granting plausible deniability through the size of the anonymity set. This is the purest architecture: zero vendor dependency, no single point of shutdown. It requires network effect to be meaningful — making it a natural fit for a shared on-chain registry on LEZ.

This prize targets architecture (3) because it aligns with Logos' design values: no trusted intermediary, no vendor that can be compelled to halt service, and a privacy guarantee that strengthens as the registry grows.

**Fake-passport attacks are real.** A government that issues the passports can forge them. Two mitigations exist in the literature: (a) a ZK similarity proof between the passport photo and a live capture (ZKML biometric matching, as demonstrated by Bionetta), and (b) a ZK participation graph where repeated, consistent participation over time builds a credential that is harder to forge at scale than a single passport. This prize requires the submission to document which mitigation (or combination) it adopts and why.

**Registration itself is a privacy leak.** Even when ballots are perfectly private, the act of *registering* can be deanonymising. In a naive design, the passport hash committed on-chain during registration is derivable by the passport-issuing authority — who holds all passport data — allowing them to enumerate exactly who registered to vote, even though they cannot see how anyone voted. In adversarial contexts (e.g., an authoritarian state targeting citizens who participated in an opposition vote), knowing *that* someone voted can be as dangerous as knowing *how* they voted.

The standard mitigation is an off-chain salt: a trusted identity provider salts the passport hash before it is committed on-chain, so the on-chain value is not directly linkable to the issuing authority's records. But this reintroduces trust: the identity provider must be trusted not to issue multiple salted hashes for the same passport (enabling Sybil attacks) and not to leak the salt (which would let the issuing authority reverse the protection). This is the architecture used in production by [iranians.vote](https://iranians.vote) via Rarimo.

The shielded passport-hash registry (architecture 3) offers a path beyond this trade-off: if the registry is large enough and used across many unrelated elections, the set of registrants for any single election is hidden within the full registry population. The issuing authority can see that a passport hash exists in the registry, but cannot determine *which* election (or elections) the holder participated in. Privacy comes from the anonymity set, not from a trusted intermediary — and it strengthens as the registry grows. Submissions must document how their design handles this registration-privacy problem, including an honest assessment of the minimum registry size required for meaningful protection.

**Censorship resistance is not optional.** MPC networks halt for sanctioned jurisdictions. States control bandwidth and can block traffic. A voting tool that depends on reachability to a specific coordinator or relay is a tool that can be switched off exactly when it is needed most. Submissions must consider transport-layer resilience — Logos Messaging provides censorship-resistant, encrypted communication that no single party controls, and is an explicit integration target.

A competitive prize is the right mechanism because the design space — nullifier derivation, passport parsing circuit, registry architecture, ballot encryption scheme, tallying mechanism, and transport-layer integration — admits many valid approaches.

## Success Criteria

- [ ] A voter holding a government-issued passport (NFC-readable, ICAO 9303 compliant) can register by generating a ZK proof of passport validity and committing a passport-derived hash to an on-chain registry, without revealing any identifying information.
- [ ] The on-chain registry enforces uniqueness: the same passport cannot register twice for the same election (double-vote prevention via nullifiers).
- [ ] A registered voter can cast a shielded ballot that is unlinkable to their registration proof, their passport, or their LEZ account by any on-chain observer or election coordinator.
- [ ] The election program determines the tally entirely on-chain, without a trusted tallier or off-chain reveal phase.
- [ ] The system is resilient to a dishonest election coordinator: a coordinator who goes offline or refuses to tally cannot prevent the result from being computed by any other party.
- [ ] Proof generation (registration and ballot) runs client-side on a standard smartphone or laptop.
- [ ] The submission documents its approach to fake-passport mitigation (ZKML biometric matching, ZK participation graph, or alternative) with a concrete threat model and known limitations.
- [ ] The submission includes a registration-privacy analysis: how the design prevents (or mitigates) the passport-issuing authority from identifying which passport holders registered, including the minimum registry size required for meaningful protection and the residual risks if the registry is small.
- [ ] A reference integration is delivered: a working demo of a complete election lifecycle (create election -> registration -> vote -> tally -> result) on LEZ testnet.
- [ ] Ballot submission and proof transmission can operate over Logos Messaging as an alternative to direct on-chain submission, enabling participation when direct chain access is blocked.
- [ ] At least 3 complete election cycles are run on LEZ testnet, each with a minimum of 5 distinct voters, with at least 2 of those elections organised by parties outside the submitting team.
- [ ] Full documentation and a clean public repository are delivered.

## Scope

### In Scope

- A Risc0 circuit proving passport validity (ICAO 9303 data group hashes + document signer verification) and deriving a per-election nullifier from passport data, without revealing any PII.
- An on-chain passport-hash registry program (Rust) that accepts registration proofs and maintains a Merkle tree of committed passport hashes.
- A LEZ election program (Rust) implementing the full lifecycle: election creation (with parameters such as candidates, duration, eligibility), ballot submission from registered voters, on-chain tallying, and result publication.
- A client-side SDK or CLI for passport scanning (NFC), proof generation, registration, and ballot casting.
- Integration with Logos Messaging for ballot submission and result distribution as a transport alternative.
- A reference integration on LEZ testnet demonstrating a complete election lifecycle.
- Documentation covering: passport parsing and verification approach, nullifier derivation scheme, registry architecture, registration-privacy analysis (issuing-authority deanonymisation risk and mitigations), ballot privacy mechanism, tallying logic, fake-passport mitigation approach and threat model, transport-layer design, security assumptions, and integration guide.

### Out of Scope

- Active Authentication (architecture 1) or external-signer (architecture 2) nullifier derivation — this prize targets the shielded registry approach.
- A production ZKML biometric matcher — if biometric matching is chosen as a fake-passport mitigation, a proof-of-concept integration is sufficient.
- Support for non-NFC identity documents (paper-only passports, national ID cards without chips).
- Coercion resistance (receipt-freeness) — while valuable, this is a distinct problem and may be the subject of a subsequent prize.
- A polished consumer UI — a working demo (CLI or minimal web interface) is sufficient.
- Ongoing maintenance or security audits beyond initial delivery.

## Potential for Subsequent λPrizes

This prize targets the shielded passport-hash registry (architecture 3) on the current LEZ testnet. Subsequent prizes may address:

- **Coercion resistance / receipt-freeness** — preventing a voter from proving how they voted to a coercer.
- **External-signer bridge** — an alternative nullifier derivation path for jurisdictions where the shielded registry has insufficient anonymity set.
- **ZK participation graph** — building long-lived identity credentials from repeated voting participation, enabling stronger Sybil resistance without biometrics.
- **Testnet migration** — should a future LEZ testnet version introduce breaking changes.
